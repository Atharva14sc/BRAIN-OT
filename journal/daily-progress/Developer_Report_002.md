# Objective

Conduct a comprehensive literature survey to understand the current state of cybersecurity solutions for Industrial Control Systems (ICS) and Operational Technology (OT), identify existing research limitations, and establish a well-defined research gap for the proposed BRAIN-OT platform.

# Background

Before beginning software development, it was necessary to understand the strengths and limitations of existing Intrusion Detection Systems, AI-based detection techniques, adaptive trust models, and industrial cybersecurity frameworks. The literature survey served as the foundation for validating the project's originality and ensuring that the proposed solution addressed an actual research need.

# Work Completed

- Studied existing Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS) and AI-assisted anomaly detection techniques.
- Analyzed the methodologies, datasets, and evaluation metrics used in current research.
- Identified recurring limitations such as static trust evaluation, high false-positive rates, and limited automated response capabilities.
- Strengthened the research gap by comparing existing approaches with the proposed Behavioral Adaptive Trust Index (BATI).
- Included benchmark datasets commonly used in ICS cybersecurity research.
- Added standard performance evaluation metrics for future implementation and validation.
- Incorporated emerging technologies relevant to industrial cybersecurity, including Explainable AI (XAI), Edge AI, Zero Trust Architecture, and Federated Learning.
- Improved the overall methodology and strengthened the justification for the proposed framework.

# Key Findings

The literature survey revealed several common limitations in existing industrial cybersecurity solutions:

- Heavy dependence on signature-based intrusion detection.
- Limited capability for continuous behavioral trust evaluation.
- Minimal adaptive decision-making after threat detection.
- Significant reliance on manual intervention for incident response.
- Lack of an integrated framework combining monitoring, adaptive trust evaluation, intrusion detection, and automated response.

These findings reinforced the need for a behavioral trust evaluation mechanism capable of continuously adapting to device behavior.

# Session Outcome

The literature survey established a strong academic foundation for BRAIN-OT. By systematically analyzing existing research, a clear research gap was identified, providing strong justification for introducing the Behavioral Adaptive Trust Index (BATI) as the project's primary research contribution.

The updated literature survey was prepared in a formal format suitable for submission to the project guide.
